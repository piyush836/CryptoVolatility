Understanding the Bitcoin Volatility Forecaster

← Back to Forecast

🎯 The Problem

Bitcoin traders, portfolio managers, and risk analysts need reliable volatility forecasts to:

  • Price options and derivatives correctly
  • Size positions appropriately
  • Manage downside risk during volatile periods
  • Execute hedging strategies

Traditional approaches like GARCH or complex machine learning models often overfit or make unrealistic assumptions about market behavior. This forecaster uses a simpler, data-driven approach.

💡 Key Finding

Naïve Volatility Persistence wins. The model simply predicts that tomorrow's 15-day volatility equals today's 15-day volatility. This approach outperforms sophisticated ML and econometric models because:

  • Volatility clustering: High volatility periods tend to follow other high volatility periods due to market microstructure and order flow dynamics
  • Information persistence: Recent market uncertainty carries over to near-term forecasts
  • No overfitting: Zero parameters to tune means zero risk of overfitting
  • Interpretability: Easy to explain to traders and stakeholders

📊 Features

Even though the naïve model doesn't use these features, they're computed for other baseline models:

past_vol_15
15-day rolling standard deviation, annualized
volume_avg_30
30-day average trading volume
high_low_ratio_5
5-day average intraday price range
past_return_7
Sum of 7-day log returns

Target Variable

target_volatility = Forward-looking 15-day annualized volatility (computed from future returns)

🏗️ Architecture

The pipeline consists of three stages:

1. Data Collection

Downloads 5+ years of daily Bitcoin (BTC-USD) price data from Yahoo Finance API. Includes: Open, High, Low, Close, Volume.

2. Feature Engineering

  • Compute log returns: log(Close[t] / Close[t-1])
  • Calculate rolling volatility (15-day window)
  • Engineer additional features (volume, price ranges, momentum)
  • Create forward-looking target (future 15-day volatility)
  • Handle missing values and outliers

3. Model Training & Evaluation

  • Train/Test split: 80% / 20% chronological
  • Compute RMSE on out-of-sample test set
  • Compare naive, XGBoost, and GARCH models
  • Select best performer (naïve wins)
Data Pipeline Flow:

Raw BTC Prices → Feature Engineering → Train/Test Split → Model Training → Evaluation → Forecast

📈 Why This Matters

This project demonstrates an important principle in quantitative finance:

  • Domain knowledge > Complexity: Understanding what volatility clustering means and why it matters is more valuable than throwing every ML algorithm at the problem
  • Simplicity is a feature: The naïve model is interpretable, maintainable, and robust to market regime changes
  • Empirical validation: We back our conclusions with data, not assumptions
  • Practical utility: Traders can use this forecast immediately to manage risk
